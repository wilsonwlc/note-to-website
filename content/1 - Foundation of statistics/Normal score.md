### Concept
- Aka **z-score** or **standard score**
- *What is it:* a value on the [[Normal distribution|standard normal distribution]]
### Definition
$$z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$$
- $x$: observed value
- $\mu$: [[Population mean|population mean]]
- $\sigma$: [[Population standard deviation|population standard deviation]]
### Interpretation
- It represents how far an observation is from the mean in units of standard deviations *i.e.* how many standard deviations $x$ is above or below the mean *e.g.*
	- $z = 0$: exactly at the mean
	- $z = 1$: 1 SD above the mean
	- $z = -1$: 1 SD below the mean
	- $z = 2$: 2 SD above the mean
- Positive normal score indicates that $x$ is greater than the mean
- Negative normal score indicates that $x$ is below the mean
### Example
- Assume $X \sim N(70, 10^{2})$
- We observe $x=90$ and have $z=\frac{90-70}{10}=2$
- So 90 has a normal score of 2, meaning it is 2 standard deviations above the mean
