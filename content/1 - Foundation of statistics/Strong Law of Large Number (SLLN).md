### Theorem
Suppose $E(\left| X_{i} \right|) < \infty$. Then 
$$
P\left( \lim_{n \to \infty} \overline{X}_{n} = \mu \right) = 1
$$
### Interpretation
- The [[Sample mean|sample mean]] converge **almost surely** towards the [[Population mean|population mean]]. It is denoted as 
$$
\overline{X}_{n} \xrightarrow{a.s.} \mu
$$
### Comparison
#### WLLN vs SLLN
- [[Weak Law of Large Number (WLLN)|WLLN]]: For sufficiently large $n$, the sample mean $\overline{X}_{n}$ is likely to be near $\mu$, but still allowing $|\overline{X}_{n}-\mu| \geq \varepsilon$ to happen an infinite number of times, though at very infrequent intervals
- [[Strong Law of Large Number (SLLN)|SLLN]]: Ensure $|\overline{X}_{n}-\mu| \geq \varepsilon$ will not happen
