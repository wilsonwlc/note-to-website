### Concept
- *What it does:* the [[Critical value|critical values]] of the [[Chi-square distribution|Chi-square distribution]] $\chi_{\alpha,df}^{2}$ for a chosen **significance level** $\alpha$ 
- *How to read it:*
	- Row: degrees of freedom, $df$ or $\nu$
	- Column: upper-tail probability (right-tail probability) or significance level, $\alpha$
### Practice
- Commonly tabulated upper-tail probabilities include 0.05, 0.025, 0.01, 0.005, 0.001
- The column headers may be labeled as upper-tail probabilities, cumulative probabilities, or percentiles, depending on the source
### Caution
- Most textbook chi-square tables report upper-tail critical values, which are particularly convenient for tests where large values of the chi-square statistic lead to rejection
- For a two-sided test involving a chi-square statistic, you need two critical values, one from each tail. 
	- The lower critical value: $\chi_{1-\frac{\alpha}{2},\nu}^{2}$ *e.g.* $\chi_{0.975,\nu}^{2}$
	- The upper critical value: $\chi_{\frac{\alpha}{2},\nu}^{2}$ *e.g.* $\chi_{0.025,\nu}^{2}$
