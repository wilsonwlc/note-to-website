### Concept
- *Purpose:* assess the presence, absence, or severity of a disease or condition *i.e.* decision making
- *Difficulty:* Answer the question "How accurate are tests used to diagnose disease?"
- *Where its data comes from:* 
	- Tests performed in a laboratory
	- Clinical information obtained from history and physical examinations
- *How its data looks like:* It is reduced to a simpler form
	- Ordinal scale
	- Dichotomy e.g. present /absent, diseased/well, abnormal/normal
- *Given the result, how to establish diagnoses:* make the medical decisions through the probability. *Reason:* Establishing diagnoses is not a perfect process
### Data
The relationship between diagnostic test result and the occurrence of disease is $$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|} \hline & \textbf{Disease} & \textbf{} \\ \textbf{Test} & \textit{Present} & \textit{Absent} \\ \hline \textit{Positive} & \text{True positive} & \text{False positive} \\ \hline \textit{Negative} & \text{False negative} & \text{True negative} \\ \hline \end{array}$$
### Metric
To describe its accuracy or performance,
- [[Sensitivity]] and [[Specificity]]
- [[Likelihood Ratio]]
### Type
- [[Golden standard]]: the most accurate diagnostic test
### Example
#### Hypertension
- The blood pressure is an interval scale measurement, ordinarily measured to the nearest 1 mmHg
- In practice, most hypertension treatment guidelines choose a particular level at which to initiate drug treatment *e.g.*
	- The World Health Organisation defines hypertension as a condition where an individual's resting blood pressure is consistently equal to or higher than 140/90 mmHg (systolic/diastolic).
	- The Joint National Committee on the Detection, Evaluation, and Treatment of Hypertension suggested the following classification on hypertensions
		- Systolic 120-139 mmHg or diastolic 80-89 mmHg: Prehypertension
		- Systolic 140-159 mmHg or diastolic 90-99 mmHg: Stage 1 hypertension
		- Systolic ≥ 160 mmHg or diastolic ≥ 100 mmHg: Stage 2 hypertension
#### Severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS)
- *Antibody tests*: It detects antibodies against SARS-CoV (SARS covonavirus). There are 2 types. (1) ELISA (Enzyme Linked ImmunoSorbant Assay) - reliable as from day 21 after the onset of clinical symptoms and signs; (2) Immunofluorescence Assays - reliable as from day 10 of illness onset
- *Molecular tests (PCR)*: It detects genetic material of the SARS virus and hence is only useful for early SARS diagnosis. The PCR based tests are known to be less **sensitive** and **specific** than the antibody tests in spite of its ability of early diagnostics. In 2003, Poon LLM developed a sensitive diagnostic test by optimising the viral RNA extraction methods and by applying real-time RT-PCR technology
- *Cell Culture*: It isolates and grows (culture) the virus in the specimens from suspected SARS patients. Once isolated, the virus must be identified as the SARS-CoV with further tests. It is a very demanding test but the only means to show the existence of a live virus. Hence, it serves as the [[Golden standard|golden standard]]
