### Concept
- *What is it:* any [[Attribute|attribute]], [[Characteristics|characteristic]] or [[Exposure|exposure]] that is statistically associated with an increased probability of a disease or other adverse health outcome
- *Type:*
	- Modifiable *e.g.* smoking, diet
	- Non-modifiable *e.g.* age, sex, genotype
- *Challenges in identifying risk factors:* 
	- The latency period between exposure and disease onset may be long, making it difficult to establish the relationship
	- The disease may be rare, resulting in insufficient observations to detect associations reliably
	- Most diseases are multifactorial, with several risk factors acting simultaneously. The effect of an individual risk factor may be small, requiring a large sample size to detect a statistically significant association
	- [[Confounding factor|Confounding]]
	- **Measurement error**
	- [[Bias|Selection bias]]
### Example
Lung cancer has the
- *Behavioural:* cigarette smoking
- *Inherited:* certain genetic variants or a family history of lung cancer
- *Environmental:* air pollution, radon exposure, asbestos exposure
### Caution
- Having or being exposed to risk factor(s) does not necessarily mean that a person will develop the disease
- A risk factor is not necessarily a **causal factor**. It may be causal, part of the causal pathway, or simply associated with the true causal factor. Therefore, an observed association does not by itself establish causation
### Comparison
#### Risk factor vs Exposure vs Attribute vs Characteristics 
- [[Risk factor|Risk factor]] is the overarching functional concept. It is anything associated with elevated risk
- [[Exposure|Exposure]], [[Attribute|attribute]], and [[Characteristics|characteristic]] describe different types or sources of those risk factors
	- Attributes are a subset of personal characteristics that are intrinsic
	- Exposures are usually (but not always) external or behavioural factors to which an individual is subjected
	- Characteristics is the broadest descriptive term and includes person, place and time variables, only some of which function as risk factors
- The same variable can be labelled differently according to the research question *e.g.* smoking can be treated as an exposure, a behavioural risk factor, or a personal characteristic
