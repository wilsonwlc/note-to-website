### Concept
- *What is exposure to a risk factor:* the presence of, or contact with, a risk factor before the occurrence of the disease or outcome of interest
### Comparison
#### Exposure vs attribute
- **Exposure** usually refers to external factors to which an individual is subjected, such as smoking, air pollution, occupational hazards, diet, or social conditions
- **Attribute** refers to intrinsic characteristics of an individual, such as age, sex, ethnicity, genetic factors, or family history
### Practice
- Exposure may be measured as a binary variable (e.g. smoker/non-smoker)
- Many exposures are naturally quantitative variables (e.g. number of cigarettes smoked per day, alcohol consumption per week, or daily coffee intake)
- For descriptive analyses, continuous exposure variables are sometimes categorised into binary or ordinal groups and summarised using contingency tables
- However, continuous exposure variables are often retained in their original form and analysed directly using statistical models to avoid loss of information
