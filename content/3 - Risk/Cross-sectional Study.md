### Concept
- *What is it:* an observational study that measures exposure(s) and outcome(s) at the same point in time *i.e.* a “snapshot” with no individual follow-up
- *Primary measure:* **Prevalence**
- *Example:* A survey of adults in a city that simultaneously records smoking status and the presence of chronic cough
- *Pros:* 
	- Quick 
	- Inexpensive
- *Cons:*
	- Cannot establish temporal sequence (and therefore causality) *e.g.* If smokers show higher prevalence of chronic cough, it is impossible to determine whether smoking preceded the cough or the cough influenced smoking behaviour
- *When to use it:* 
	- Estimating disease or exposure prevalence
	- Assessing the health status of a population at a specific time
### Comparison
#### Prospective cohort vs Retrospective cohort vs Case-Control vs Cross-Sectional
| Study Type           | Selection Based On | Time Direction                              | Follow-up?              | Main Measure             |
| -------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Prospective cohort   | Exposure status    | Forward                                     | Yes                     | Risk, RR, Incidence rate |
| Retrospective cohort | Exposure status    | Historical                                  | No new follow-up needed | Risk, RR, Incidence rate |
| Case-Control         | Disease status     | Looks backwards to assess previous exposure | No                      | OR                       |
| Cross-Sectional      | Target population  | Single time point                           | No                      | Prevalence               |
